What is a Square Root?

Imagine you have an army of square blocks and you line them up to make one giant square shape. The **Square Root** is simply the length of **one side** of that perfect square! If you have 25 blocks total, they form a 5x5 grid, so the square root of 25 is 5!

Method 1

Prime Factorization Using Division

This method breaks down a big number into its building blocks (prime numbers) by continuous division.

Step-by-Step Guide for 1764:

  • Divide by the smallest prime number (2, 3, 5, 7, …) that divides evenly into the number.
  • Keep dividing the new answers until you reach 1.
  • Group the prime factors into pairs of identical numbers.
  • Take one number from each pair and multiply them to get your final square root!

Let's Solve It:

1764 ÷ 2 = 882 (Even number, divide by 2)

882 ÷ 2 = 441 (Even number, divide by 2)

441 ÷ 3 = 147 (Sum of digits is 4+4+1=9, divisible by 3)

147 ÷ 3 = 49 (49 is 7 × 7)

49 ÷ 7 = 7

7 ÷ 7 = 1

Now, write out all the prime factors we used:

1764 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 3 × 7 × 7

Next, group them into pairs:

  • Pair of twos: (2 × 2) → Take one 2
  • Pair of threes: (3 × 3) → Take one 3
  • Pair of sevens: (7 × 7) → Take one 7

Multiply the single numbers from each pair:

"Square root" = 2 × 3 × 7 = 42

⚠️ Constraints & Weaknesses:

  • Takes a long time for big numbers.
  • If the number splits into huge prime factors (like 79 × 79), it becomes very hard to guess the divisor without a calculator!
Method 2

Vedic Maths Mental Calculation Trick ⚡

Vedic mathematics offers a lightning-fast trick to find the square root of a 4-digit perfect square in your head without doing long division!

Step-by-Step Guide for 1764:

Step 1: Find the Units Digit

Look at the last digit of your number. For 1764, the last digit is 4. Ask yourself: Which single-digit numbers, when multiplied by themselves, end in 4?

2 × 2 = 4

8 × 8 = 64 (ends in 4)

So, our units digit is either 2 or 8.

Step 2: Find the Tens Digit

Ignore the last two digits (64) and look at the remaining part: 17. Find which two perfect square numbers 17 sits between:

4^2 = 16

5^2 = 25

Since 17 is between 16 and 25, we always pick the smaller base number, which is 4. This means our tens digit is 4.

Now, our answer is either 42 or 48.

Step 3: Choose the Correct Number

Take the tens digit (4), multiply it by the very next counting number (5):

4 × 5 = 20

Compare our remaining number (17) with this result (20):

Since 17 is smaller than 20, we pick the smaller option between 42 and 48.

Therefore, the square root of 1764 is 42!

⚠️ Constraints & Weaknesses:

  • **ONLY** works on absolute perfect squares! If you try this trick on 250, it gives a completely wrong answer.
  • Maxes out safely at 4-digit squares.

What is a Cube Root?

Think 3D! If you build a solid Rubik's Cube style block out of tiny building cubes, the **Cube Root** is the exact measure of its **width, height, and depth**. If your 3D structure uses 64 small cubes total, it's a 4x4x4 cube layout, making the cube root of 64 exactly 4!

Method 1

Prime Factorization Using Division

Just like finding square roots, this method breaks down a number into its prime factors. However, because it is a cube root, we group the numbers into triplets (groups of 3) instead of pairs.

Step-by-Step Guide for 13824:

  • Divide continuously by the smallest prime numbers (2, 3, …) until you reach 1.
  • Group identical prime factors into triplets (sets of 3).
  • Take one number from each triplet and multiply them together.

Let's Solve It:

13824 ÷ 2 = 6912

6912 ÷ 2 = 3456

3456 ÷ 2 = 1728

1728 ÷ 2 = 864

864 ÷ 2 = 432

432 ÷ 2 = 216

216 ÷ 2 = 108

108 ÷ 2 = 54

54 ÷ 2 = 27

27 ÷ 3 = 9

9 ÷ 3 = 3

3 ÷ 3 = 1

Now, write out all the prime factors:

13824 = (2×2×2) × (2×2×2) × (2×2×2) × (3×3×3)

Next, group them into triplets:

  • First triplet of twos: (2×2×2) → Take one 2
  • Second triplet of twos: (2×2×2) → Take one 2
  • Third triplet of twos: (2×2×2) → Take one 2
  • Triplet of threes: (3×3×3) → Take one 3

Multiply the single numbers from each set:

"Cube root" = 2 × 2 × 2 × 3 = 24

⚠️ Constraints & Weaknesses:

  • Division columns become extremely long and error-prone very quickly.
  • Stalls completely if the prime factors are large double-digit primes.
Method 2

Vedic Maths Mental Calculation Trick 🧙‍♂️

Vedic Maths makes finding cube roots even faster than square roots! This is because every single digit from 0 to 9 produces a unique last digit when cubed.

Handy Reference Table (Units Digits of Cubes):

1^3 = 1 (ends in 1)

2^3 = 8 (ends in 8)

3^3 = 27 (ends in 7)

4^3 = 64 (ends in 4)

5^3 = 125 (ends in 5)

6^3 = 216 (ends in 6)

7^3 = 343 (ends in 3)

8^3 = 512 (ends in 2)

9^3 = 729 (ends in 9)

Cool pattern to remember: 1, 4, 5, 6, 9 stay the same! 2 swaps with 8, and 3 swaps with 7.

Step-by-Step Guide for 13824:

Step 1: Find the Units Digit

Look at the last digit of 13824, which is 4.

  • Looking at our table, the only single digit whose cube ends in 4 is 4 (4^3=64).
  • Therefore, our units digit is 4.

Step 2: Find the Tens Digit

Cross out the last three digits (824) and look at the remaining part: 13. Find which two perfect cubes 13 sits between:

2^3 = 8

3^3 = 27

Since 13 lies between 8 and 27, always pick the smaller base number, which is 2.

Therefore, our tens digit is 2.

Combining both digits gives 24!
∛13824 = 24

⚠️ Constraints & Weaknesses:

  • Fails entirely on numbers that aren't exact perfect cubes.
  • Requires memorizing the first 9 cubes (1³ to 9³).
